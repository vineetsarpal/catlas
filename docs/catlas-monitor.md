# Monitor

Open `/?mode=monitor` or `exposure.html?mode=monitor`, or choose **Monitor** in the top navigation. Monitor, Exposure and Recovery share the header, with a dedicated Portfolios destination and an active portfolio selector. Catlas keeps its MapLibre map, appearance controls, geography and existing Exposure/Recovery workspaces. Earthquake and cyclone discovery need no backend or provider key. Wind requires the Catlas backend; vessel monitoring also uses it.

Monitor discovers the past 24 hours of USGS earthquakes and NOAA NHC/CPHC cyclone advisories. The left tool rail opens **Attention**, **Locations**, **Watchlist**, **Feeds**, and **Layers** options. Selected items open in the right details panel; the map stays in the center. On mobile, opening options closes details and selecting an item closes options. The map retains its **Appearance** menu. Layers contains switches for earthquakes, tropical cyclones, insured locations and wind, plus independent fire/warning monitoring and map-visibility controls. **Fit portfolio** appears under Insured locations only while that layer is enabled. Appearance contains Light/Dark map, Globe view (on by default; off returns to a flat map with horizontal panning and a zoom-out limit that avoids repeated regions), borders and place labels. Each switch reveals its related details only while enabled; wind includes pause, shading and forecast-time controls. **Feeds** in Monitor details exposes per-feed check times/errors. Run time, valid time, advisory text and source-file timestamps are distinct. The backend downloads only the surface U/V components with bounded HTTP ranges from a single GFS 1° forecast object and decodes them in a Node worker. The browser receives validated numeric vectors and renders them; it downloads no decoder or WASM assets. It is forecast context, not a location-level hazard assessment.

The attention list ranks published warning/perimeter intersections before proximity matches and then by flagged location count. **Events relevant to portfolio locations** retains known matches and items whose missing boundary prevents screening. Earthquakes and cyclones retain 250 km point proximity; fires use the adjustable fire distance. These are investigation attention flags, not damage assessments. A cyclone center or uncertainty cone does not describe its full hazard footprint. Coverage is Atlantic and eastern/central North Pacific, not worldwide cyclones. Location points remain unassessed in Monitor and use a neutral portfolio color rather than exposure intensity colors.

Watch events or location points, switch to **Watchlist**, and optionally show watched items only on the map. Watchlist references and monitoring layer/filter preferences persist in localStorage on this browser and origin, with a 100-item watchlist limit. Events outside the rolling feed or disabled layers remain listed as unavailable watch references; they are not silently promoted to current observations. A local storage failure is reported and leaves session operation usable. The selected forecast time is session state and defaults to the provider's advertised current time after reload.

Selecting an earthquake offers **Capture evidence & investigate exposure**. Successful capture explicitly opens an exposure investigation using the loaded portfolio; missing ShakeMap produces unknown intensity with the existing explanation. Failed capture preserves the prior view and investigation. Source polling, forecast selection, watching and map filtering never rewrite an existing investigation or recovery evaluation. Frozen cases and reviewed packets remain in their existing storage and version lifecycle.

Monitor polls only while active, visible and online. Failed refreshes retain prior observations with unavailable/stale messaging; offline mode pauses acquisition. Wind display is hidden when offline or when a new vector field is pending/unavailable, preventing a previous field from being labelled with a newly selected time. Static geography and existing offline investigation workflows remain usable; no live-feed availability is promised offline. Feed state is retained during mode switches in the session, not persisted as evidence after reload.

Manage named insured-location inventories through **Portfolios**. Monitor uses the active selection, which persists in this browser; historical exposure snapshots remain separate. The vessel watch portfolio is a separate monitoring inventory. The local backend supports viewport vessel monitoring and vessel-list import (CAT-35). Cargo relationships, marine exposure assessments and shared accounts/storage remain deferred. The implementation is tracked in CAT-36.

Public source links, provider terms and the pinned God's Eye View reference/notice are in [monitor sources](../frontend/public/monitor-sources.md).

## Wind flow trial

Wind is now drawn as geographic streamlines on Catlas’s existing MapLibre map. Presentation in the left Layers panel includes a **Pause wind motion** preference, optional **Show wind speed shading**, a knots legend and the model run/forecast valid time. Trail motion is accelerated for readability and never advances the forecast time. Reduced-motion preferences show static streamlines. The draw loop stops while the page is hidden, the map is moving, the layer is off, offline, or another mode is selected; paths are recalculated after the camera settles. Both regional Mercator and globe views use the same numerical field.

Catalog checks use a five-hour publication lag and can fall back one six-hour cycle if an inventory is missing. The time selector offers GFS three-hour steps through 48 hours; a missing forecast is reported as unavailable. The decoder verifies the selected run, valid time, component names, 10 m level, units, matching grids and scan direction. Full-file responses and oversized/short range bodies are rejected. The backend caches four decoded fields; the browser retains the most recently received field for up to five minutes; decoded wind is not saved as investigation evidence. Switching the forecast cancels the browser request; backend work can still finish and populate its bounded cache.

Wind acquisition, GRIB decoding and validation run only on the backend. Standalone and packaged offline builds show a disabled Wind switch with **Wind requires the Catlas backend.** Existing saved wind preferences cannot trigger browser decoding or direct NOAA wind requests. A configured backend failure is reported as unavailable; **Retry wind forecasts** retries discovery and vector loading without a browser fallback.

## Optional local backend

CAT-35 provides backend wind decoding and viewport vessel monitoring. See [backend setup](catlas-backend.md). With `VITE_CATLAS_BACKEND=true`, wind grids are fetched from the local backend. No browser decoder is shipped. The vessel layer offers Portfolio / All vessels, CSV import, recent trails and per-position report times. Browser-only and offline builds remain the default. Public deployment is deferred.

## US fire and weather attention

The optional local backend now supplies three independent monitoring feeds: NASA FIRMS VIIRS NOAA-20/NOAA-21 detections, NIFC WFIGS current incident perimeters, and a property-focused selection of NWS watches, warnings and advisories. FIRMS uses a broad US regional envelope including Alaska and Hawaii; neighboring countries can appear in that envelope. These feeds are regional, incomplete and distinct from global hazard coverage. FIRMS requires `FIRMS_MAP_KEY` in `backend/.env`; perimeters and warnings need no provider key. All three require `VITE_CATLAS_BACKEND=true` and the running backend. Standalone/offline builds explain their unavailability.

The left Layers panel separates **Monitor** switches from **Show on map** checkboxes. All configured feeds monitor by default. Warning areas and perimeters are initially visible, while hotspot dots are hidden. Hiding a layer leaves its portfolio attention results available; disabling monitoring stops client acquisition and removes that feed from attention. Requests run only while Monitor is active, visible and online. FIRMS clients check every ten minutes against a thirty-minute backend cache; perimeters every five minutes; warnings every minute. Backend cache acquisition occurs on demand, not through a background monitor or notification service.

Portfolio attention combines existing events with named fire incidents, per-location groups of nearby satellite detections and weather alerts. A hazard selector filters the list. Published-area intersections rank ahead of proximity matches; the list does not create a severity score across hazards. Fire proximity defaults to 10 km and can be changed from 0 to 100 km. Earthquake/cyclone point proximity remains 250 km. Perimeter distance is measured to the published boundary; warning screening uses the supplied polygon or complete official NWS zone boundaries. Polygon holes, multipolygons and dateline crossings are handled. Fuller, unsimplified WFIGS geometry is used for screening rather than God's Eye View's display-generalized boundaries.

Each fire/warning item explains its screening reason and reports distinct flagged location counts, TIV separately by currency, and unknown-value counts. These are attention flags, not physical damage, insured loss or a contractual recovery. A location can appear under multiple events; per-event values must not be summed as a portfolio loss. Multiple satellite observations can describe one fire. No named incident is inferred from a group of nearby detections, and satellite points are not burned-area polygons. Raw hotspot dots retain satellite detection coordinates; the attention groups refer to portfolio locations.

FIRMS observations expire after a rolling 24 hours, including when cached data is served during an outage. Perimeters display source-update time and age independently of feed acquisition time; no arbitrary age cutoff establishes whether a fire remains active. Current WFIGS publication is not a verified complete active-incident census. Invalid or missing boundaries remain visibly unscreened. Expired/cancelled warnings leave active attention. Watches follow incident identifiers and NWS update/reference chains, remain browser-local, and retain ended/unavailable references. This release stores no hazard evidence history or new Exposure investigation type.

Partial-source warnings, rejected records and stale retained evidence appear in Feeds. The attention panel signals degraded coverage. Missing geometry means screening unavailable rather than zero matching locations. Feed refreshes and current attention never change existing Exposure or Recovery evidence versions.
